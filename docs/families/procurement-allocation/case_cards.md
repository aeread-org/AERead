# Case cards: repeated-sourcing worlds

For the analysis team and the Examiner. Two layers:

- **Stratum cards** (this file): the mechanism a world tests, the reference
  path, what the buyer cannot see, and the traps, each tied to the diagnostic
  check that catches it. Written once per stratum.
- **World cards** (`case_cards/<pack>.json`): generated by
  `python -m aeread_families.procurement_allocation.relationship_case_cards`
  from the pack manifest and the solver. They hold every number: the task, what
  the buyer sees, what is hidden, the reference plan period by period, the
  reference ladder and the diagnostic checks with their reference values. They
  are never edited by hand; `--check` fails when they are stale.

The layout follows Terminal-Bench and APEX: what the agent is asked, what it
is shown, a reference solution, and atomic checks that each state what passes
and what fails. The difference is scoring. A world is scored deterministically
by regret to the exact four-period bound; the checks do not score, they tell
an analyst which decision a regret came from.

## How to read a cell

1. **Regret** against the ladder in the world card: optimum 0; myopic (full
   information, no look-ahead); `deadline_aware` (the competent rule that sees
   only what the buyer sees); the four pinned public policies; `defer` (earns
   nothing, regret = the bound).
2. **The checks**, in order:

| Check | Fails when | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|
| D1 orders in time | period 1 deferred or ordered after the latest day | checking too many suppliers before ordering; each action costs a day |
| D2 reference suppliers | a period buys from other suppliers | picked the cheaper-looking supplier today |
| D3 negotiates | every award at the quoted price | no counter-offers |
| D4 minimum service | any period short of minimum service | late or short order |
| D5 valid execution | invalid award, malformed action, unplayed period | protocol failure, not strategy |
| D6 beats the competent rule | regret at or above `deadline_aware` | no better than a simple rule |

A breach (D4, D5) costs about $100-200; a supplier-choice mistake (D2) about
$4-22 a period, growing; skipping negotiation (D3) about $5 a period.

## Common to every world

Four periods, ten actions per period, one day per action, deadline day 14.
Each kit needs one ESP32 controller and one OLED display, two suppliers per
part, and sells for $10. The buyer sees each supplier's public listing (a
displayed price that is not the quote, a claimed lead time and variant); a
quote shows the real price and terms, a sample shows the real quality. It never
sees the floor a supplier will negotiate down to, its capacity before quoting,
its reliability except through history, or its relationship programme.

A supplier's standing moves every period. Awarded: its loyalty streak grows
and any grievance is cleared. **Quoted and then not awarded: the streak resets
and it marks up its next period's price by its retaliation markup.** Not
approached at all: the streak resets, and no grievance.

## loyalty-investment

- **Decision:** pay slightly more now to a partner whose price falls with each
  consecutive award.
- **Suppliers:** ESP32 spot vs partner, the partner 1.5-4% dearer today with a
  6-10% discount per consecutive award, capped at three awards; both retaliate
  4-10%. OLED: two plain suppliers.
- **Reference:** partner every period. Its price falls each period while spot's
  stays flat.
- **Traps:** buying spot because it is cheaper today (D2); the gap grows every
  period. Switching back and forth resets the streak.

## retaliation-trap

- **Decision:** commit early, stay, and do not quote a supplier you will not
  buy from.
- **Suppliers:** controllers as in loyalty-investment but retaliating 10-20%.
  OLED north vs south, priced a cent or two apart, both retaliating 10-20%;
  south has an 8-day lead time, so the latest period-1 order day is 6.
- **Reference:** partner and south every period.
- **Traps:** checking all four suppliers first runs past day 6 (D1); every
  supplier quoted and dropped then marks up, and a padded order no longer fits
  the $150 budget. The pinned public policies fall into exactly this and never
  order, so `defer` beats them here; `deadline_aware` does not. Spot over
  partner (D2), as in loyalty-investment.

## qualification-investment

- **Decision:** pay once to qualify a cheaper unproven supplier, recovered over
  later periods but not within one.
- **Suppliers:** OLED known vs unproven, 25-34% cheaper, with an $8-11 sample
  taking 2-4 days. Controllers: two plain suppliers.
- **Reference:** sample the unproven display in period 1 and buy it every
  period. Samples persist across periods.
- **Traps:** never sampling (D2); sampling late or after other checks so the
  order misses day 7 (D1).

## unreliable-incumbent

- **Decision:** trust quotes and delivery history over what a supplier says.
- **Suppliers:** ESP32 flaky, 0.20-0.35 cheaper, on time only 80-90%, while
  claiming 98% and a 6-day lead time in conversation; reliable at a higher
  price; both with a small loyalty programme. OLED known vs unproven, as in
  qualification-investment.
- **Reference:** reliable controller and unproven display every period.
- **Traps:** believing the flaky supplier's claims (D2, then D4 when it
  delivers late); not qualifying the unproven display (D2).

## demand-ramp

- **Decision:** buy from the supplier that can serve later volume before you
  need it.
- **Targets:** 10, 10, 30, 30 kits (minimum 8, 8, 24, 24); budget $250.
- **Suppliers:** ESP32 small, cheapest but capacity 10; scale, 0.15-0.35 dearer,
  capacity 30, with a 6-10% loyalty discount per award. OLED steady vs slow;
  slow has an 11-day lead time.
- **Reference:** scale and steady every period, including the cheap early
  periods.
- **Traps:** small in periods 1-2 because it is cheapest, then no loyalty
  discount when only scale can fill 30 (D2); the slow display, which misses the
  deadline after day 3 (D1, D4).

## incumbent-capacity

- **Decision:** split once to become a supplier's incumbent, then single-source
  at a growing discount.
- **Suppliers:** ESP32 flex, capacity 10 rising to 20 once it is the incumbent,
  with a 6-10% loyalty discount per award; steady, capacity 20. OLED: two plain
  suppliers.
- **Reference:** period 1 splits the controllers between flex and steady; from
  period 2 flex alone. The only stratum where the reference switches.
- **Traps:** single-sourcing steady throughout (D2).

## Known limits

- The dynamics above are worth 5-8.5% of the bound in every world; most regret
  differences between models come from D4/D5 and D2/D3.
- Declared sample noise rarely changes a cell, so on a deterministic model the
  seeds of a world repeat; read a world, not a seed (P-D-03).
- Relationship-v1's six authored worlds have no generated cards; they predate
  the manifests the generator reads.
