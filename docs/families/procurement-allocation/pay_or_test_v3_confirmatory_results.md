# Pay or test v3: confirmatory and diagnostic results

Read out as [the plan](pay_or_test_v3_confirmatory_plan.md) declares; evidence in
`evidence/procurement_allocation/pay_or_test_v3_confirmatory/`. 100 of 100 plays completed, every one replayed.

| Arm | Subject | Worlds | Plays touching a newcomer | Gap closed, pay to best | Loss a world (pay the premium) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| A, confirmatory | Sol 6.1 | 20 option-value | 26 of 40 | **35.3%, 95% t interval [18.4, 52.2]**, bootstrap [20.6, 51.1]; t = 4.38, p = 0.0003 | $32.51 ($51.25) |
| B, control | Opus 5.5 | the same 20 | 0 of 40 | 0% | $51.25 ($51.25) |
| B, odds twins | Opus 5.5 | 20 twins at v1's odds | 0 of 20 | 0% | pays in every twin |

**Arm A holds.** In 20 fresh worlds where the best first move is a test worth only what it reveals, and where the
one-step rule pays the premium, Sol 6.1 closes a positive share of the gap between paying and the best policy. The
interval's lower end, 18.4%, is above zero. No world is missing. Sol touched a newcomer in 26 of 40 plays, and its first
information request went to a newcomer in 10. It met 25 unanswered quotes and asked again after 12 of them. It looked a
source up in 4 plays, settling a kind's sharing in 3.

**What arm A does not show.** The kind-blind shortcut that admission screens (sample the newcomer with the cheapest
sample first, then look one test ahead) closes 33.4% of the gap per world on these 20 worlds and loses $33.46, against
Sol's 35.3% and $32.51. The admission screen only required it to lose at least $10 to the best policy. So Sol's share is
no more than that habit reaches: the declared claim, a positive share, holds, but it does not show Sol reasoning about
shared sources. On the 6 pilot worlds the same shortcut closes 7.6%. No comparison with it was declared (incident row
P-J-06). Sol does not play that habit, though. It opens with the shortcut's move in 2 of 40 plays, and its per-world
shares correlate with the shortcut's at 0.21 (10 worlds more than 0.1 above it, 10 below), so the equal means are a
coincidence of averages. Its tests are price-led: the first newcomer it touches is the cheapest-priced in 19 of 26
plays. The moves the case is built around are rare: a second newcomer in 7 of 40 plays, a lookup in 4, a kind's
sharing settled in 3.

**Arm B: the odds alone do not explain why Opus never tests.** Under version 1's cohort odds every twin's best first
move goes to a newcomer, testing beats paying by 10% to 102%, and the one-step rule tests in 18 of 20. Opus still
paid the premium in all 20. Across version 3, Opus has now paid the premium without touching a newcomer in 84 of 84
plays (pilot 24, control 40, twins 20).

**What follows.** The cause is in what version 3 shares with version 2 and not with version 1: Opus also never tested
on v2's pack (0 of 12, design note probe). The candidates are the brief's shared-source and answer-rate paragraphs, the
worlds' structure (record-drawn prices, the shared source itself), and the answer rate. Each is a hypothesis for a
declared one-factor test, not a finding. Arm B's reading was declared before the plays, and it is all that is
claimed here.
