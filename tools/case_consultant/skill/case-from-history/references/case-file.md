# The case file (protocol case-consultant/1)

Generated from `src/core.js` by `build_skill.mjs`. Do not edit by hand.

Twelve sections. Each lists what to file, what it becomes in a built case, and what to ask when the history does not say.

## The expert

Becomes: Where every answer comes from.

Needs: Line of work, role and experience.

- **Line of work**
- **Role**
- **Experience**

Ask, if the history is silent: "To start: what is your line of work, what is your role in it, and how long have you done it?"

Press on:
- Keep this part to one or two exchanges.

## Decision-maker and goal

Becomes: The seat being tested, and what it is scored on.

Needs: Who decides, the options that were open, and what counts as it going well.

- **Who decides**
- **The decision, in one sentence**
- **Options on the table** (list)
- **What they are trying to achieve**
- **The last real instance**
- **How often it comes up**

Ask, if the history is silent: "Pick one decision from your work that is hard to get right, one where a capable newcomer would often choose worse than you do. Tell me about the last time you made it: what was the situation, what could you have done, and what did you choose?"

Press on:
- If they describe more than one decision, ask which one to keep.
- If they describe what usually happens, ask for the last real instance.
- If only one option is named, ask what else they could have done.
- If the goal is vague, ask what would count as it going well in terms someone could check.

## Other parties

Becomes: Other seats, scripted or played.

Needs: Everyone else who acts, what each is after, and how each reacts.

- **Nobody else acts** (yes or no)
- **Parties** (table: Who | Wants | Knows that the decider does not | Can do | How they react)

Ask, if the history is silent: "Who else takes part in this decision, across the table or alongside you? For each one, what are they after?"

Press on:
- A party with no stated aim.
- Whether anyone can mislead or hold something back.
- Rivals after the same thing, and what happens if the expert delays.

## Known and hidden

Becomes: What the seat is shown, and the state it is not shown.

Needs: What is in view at the moment of choice, what is not, and when the hidden part comes out.

- **Visible at the start** (table: What | Comes from | How far it is trusted)
- **Hidden** (table: What | Who knows it | When it comes out | Why it matters)
- **What the visible hints about the hidden** (list)

Ask, if the history is silent: "At the moment you have to choose, what do you have in front of you? And what would you most like to know that you cannot see?"

Press on:
- Whether something visible is a claim by someone with an interest or a checked fact.
- Whether the hidden thing is ever revealed afterwards, and when.
- How strongly a visible sign goes with the hidden state: of ten cases with the sign, how many turn out that way.

## Moves, costs and limits

Becomes: The closed list of actions, their prices, the budget and the end conditions.

Needs: Each step open before committing, its cost in money and time, the deadline, and the step that cannot be undone.

- **Steps before committing** (table: Step | What it does | Money | Time | Limits and order)
- **Budget**
- **Deadline, and what happens when it passes**
- **The step that cannot be undone**
- **What undoing it costs**
- **What stops you checking everything**

Ask, if the history is silent: "Before you commit, what can you actually do? List each step open to you, such as asking, checking, testing, waiting or negotiating, and say what each costs in money and in time."

Press on:
- A step with no cost in money or time.
- What cannot be undone, and what undoing costs.
- If nothing stops them checking everything, ask what runs short: time, money, goodwill or attention.

## What moves reveal

Becomes: What each action returns, and its error rates.

Needs: What each check returns, how many bad cases of ten it catches, and how many good ones it wrongly flags.

- **Checks** (table: Check | What comes back | Bad cases caught, of ten | Good cases wrongly flagged, of ten | Wait | Can it be gamed)

Ask, if the history is silent: "Take the checks you just listed, one at a time. What comes back, and how far can you trust it? Out of ten bad cases, how many would it catch? Out of ten good ones, how many would it wrongly flag?"

Press on:
- An error rate given in one direction only.
- Whether the result can be gamed by the other side.
- Whether a second check adds anything after the first.

## Accounting

Becomes: The score, in money.

Needs: What is added up and taken away afterwards, and what a good and a bad outcome are worth.

- **Counts as gain** (list)
- **Counts as cost or loss** (list)
- **A good outcome is worth**
- **A bad outcome costs**
- **Size of a typical case**
- **How long before you know**
- **What is hard to put in money**

Ask, if the history is silent: "When it is all over, how do you tell, in money, whether you chose well? Walk me through what you add up and what you take away."

Press on:
- An outcome described as reputation, trust or relationship: ask what it costs when it goes wrong.
- Consequences that arrive later.
- The size of a typical case, so the checks can be compared with the stakes.

## Tension

Becomes: The hard cases: worlds on each side of the break-even.

Needs: What pulls each way, the one thing that flips the choice, and the level at which it flips.

- **What pulls each way**
- **What would flip the choice**
- **The level at which it flips**
- **Cases of ten that sit near the line**

Ask, if the history is silent: "Go back to the case you described. What would have had to be different for you to choose the other option?"

Press on:
- If nothing would flip it, ask about a time a capable colleague chose differently.
- A flip with no level: ask at what number it tips.
- How often real cases fall near that line.

## Variation

Becomes: The generator's ranges, and how often each occurs.

Needs: What differs from case to case, its range, how common each end is, and what moves together.

- **What differs between cases** (table: What changes | Range | How common | Changes the right choice | Moves together with)
- **What never changes** (list)

Ask, if the history is silent: "Across all the times you have faced this decision, what changes from one case to the next? For each thing that changes, give me its range and how common each end is."

Press on:
- A range with no frequency.
- Which things move together.
- Rare cases that matter a lot.
- In each row, changes_choice must begin with yes or no.

## Yardsticks and typical mistakes

Becomes: Reference players (no effort, newcomer rule, professional rule, full knowledge) and failure labels.

Needs: The default, the newcomer's rule and where it fails, the professional's rule, and the usual mistakes.

- **No effort: the default, and how it turns out**
- **What a capable newcomer does**
- **Where that goes wrong, and how often it still works**
- **The professional's rule**
- **With everything hidden in view**
- **Money between newcomer and professional**
- **Typical mistakes** (table: Mistake | What it costs | How to spot it from outside)

Ask, if the history is silent: "Three short ones. If you made no effort and took the default, what would happen? What does a capable newcomer usually do, and where does it go wrong? And what do you do differently, put as a rule you could hand to someone?"

Press on:
- An approach described as judgment or feel: ask what they look at first and what they do when they see it.
- How often the newcomer's approach still works, of ten.
- What they would do with everything hidden in view, and how much better that is.

## Worked situations

Becomes: Golden cases the environment must reproduce.

Needs: Three situations with their numbers: one where the obvious choice is right, one where it is wrong, one that is close.

- **Situations** (table: Kind | Situation | Numbers | Choice | Why | What would change it)

Ask, if the history is silent: "Last part. Give me three specific situations with their numbers: one where the obvious choice is right, one where it is wrong, and one that is close. For each, what would you do, and why?"

Press on:
- A situation without numbers.
- A choice that disagrees with the rule or the flip level filed earlier: that is a contradiction to raise.
- Set kind to one of: obvious right, obvious wrong, close.

## What we did not ask

Becomes: A warning that the template itself is missing something.

Needs: Anything that matters in this decision and was never asked about.

- **Matters, but was never asked** (list)

Ask, if the history is silent: "One last question before I write this up. What matters in this decision that I have not asked about?"

Press on:
- If something new comes up, ask once what it costs or how often it matters.
- If the expert says nothing is missing, file one entry in notes.missed saying so, and move on.

## Numbers

Needs: Give every figure that varies a lowest, a usual and a highest, and mark where each one comes from.

One row per figure: name, unit, low, usual, high, source (`data`, `experience` or `guess`), note.

Ask: "Now the figures you gave me along the way. First, where they come from: which of them could you look up in records, and which are guesses? I will take the rest as your experience."

## Gap kinds

Label every open question with one of these:

- `scope`: more than one decision
- `actual`: what actually happened
- `vague`: a word where a count belongs
- `range`: a number without a range
- `source`: where a number comes from
- `cost`: a step without a cost
- `error_rate`: how often a check is wrong
- `aim`: what someone is after
- `money`: an outcome not yet in money
- `flip`: what would change the choice
- `rule`: a rule someone could follow
- `contradiction`: two answers that disagree
- `heard`: checking what I heard
- `missing`: something still missing
