\section{Case specifications}\label{case-specifications}

Following TERMS-Bench \citep{zhang2026termsbench}, each case is written as an environment Γ (outcomes, private types and their prior, payoffs), a
protocol (seats, order of play, actions, what each seat observes, termination), and fixed
counterpart policies (Appendix B). Refund is not specified here. Symbols are local to each case;
each case's table lists them, with the kind of quantity each is. The evaluated seat is subscript A, a counterpart B. In controlled comparisons, the
prior and counterpart policies are fixed across evaluated agents; head-to-head role swaps change
the opponent as well, so they compare pairings. Agent configurations also differ in the settings
recorded in Appendix H. The reference policies of Appendix C are computed in the same Γ.

\textbf{Notation used across cases.} 𝟙{[}·{]} (or 𝟙(·)) is 1 when the condition inside holds and 0 otherwise. U(a, b) is a
uniform draw between a and b. 𝔼 is an expectation over the outcomes the case lists, and ⌊x⌋ rounds x down. D is each
case's loss symbol: the lemon loss (\$1,000) in Housing, the value of supplying nothing (−\$600) in Procurement.
Each case's table defines its other symbols, and Appendices C and D point back to it when they reuse one.

\textbf{Terms used across the cases.} A \emph{world} is one fully specified instance of a case (its parameters and hidden
types), fixed by a seed. A \emph{pack} is a published set of worlds, regenerated from their seeds. \emph{Admission} is the
rule, computed before any agent plays, that decides which generated worlds enter a pack. A \emph{seat} is a player
position (the tenant, a landlord, the buyer, the integrator); the evaluated agent holds one seat and scripted
policies or other agents hold the rest. A \emph{cell} is one play of one world under one condition (in Housing, one
world and one landlord kind). An \emph{identity} (such as v14 or v15) names a frozen campaign: its pack, brief,
counterparts and controls; changing any of them makes a new identity. A comparison is \emph{declared} when its plan and
analysis were committed before any of its cells ran. A \emph{gate} is one of the six quality-control stages of Section 6
(0 to 5).

\subsection{Housing: lemons with outside demand (identities v14 and v15)}\label{housing-lemons-with-outside-demand-identities-v14-and-v15}

v15 plays the same rules. Its brief states the landlords' pricing rule, its worlds come from a screened pack (Appendix E.2), and its holdout's outside demand leaves at 0.3 a round.

\textbf{Environment.} A world seed w fixes four listings ℓ ∈ \{0, 1, 2, 3\} and six tenant seats.
Seat 0 is the evaluated tenant; seat i ∈ \{1, \ldots, 4\} is outside demand for listing i − 1; seat 5
never acts. Exactly m = 2 listings are lemons, Λ ⊂ \{0, \ldots, 3\}, drawn uniformly from a random
stream separate from the one that sets values, asks and costs. Seat 0 signs at most one lease
(ℓ, rent), or none. Its payoff after signing ℓ with k inspections is

u\_A = v\_ℓ − D·𝟙{[}ℓ ∈ Λ{]} − rent − c·k, and u\_A = −c·k if it signs nothing,

where D is the lemon loss and c the inspection fee (Table A.1).

Seat 0 privately knows v\_ℓ, its value for each listing if sound. Each landlord privately knows
its listing's quality and cost. The prior is uniform over the six possible lemon pairs. The
landlord kind is not drawn: both kinds are played on every world.

Every landlord has a target rent: it accepts any offer at or above it and answers a lower offer with a counter at
it (B.1). The two kinds differ only in where the target comes from. A \emph{leaky} landlord prices off its own cost; a
lemon costs its landlord \$1,000 less, so a lemon's landlord will rent for about \$1,000 less than a sound listing's,
and an offer between the two rents is accepted only by a lemon's landlord: the acceptance itself shows that the
listing is a lemon. A \emph{pooled} landlord prices every listing as if it were sound, so its replies say nothing about
quality. Each world is played once under each kind.

\textbf{Protocol.} Each round r = 0, 1, 2 has four phases. (1) \emph{Inspect}: seat 0 may pay c to
inspect one open listing it has not inspected; the quality appears in its observation at once.
(2) \emph{Contact}: seat 0 makes one offer (ℓ, x) with ℓ open and x ≥ 0, or passes; offering again on
a listing it walked from is legal. (3) \emph{Respond}: each landlord applies its policy (B.1) to its
inbox; each listing creates at most one binding hold. (4) \emph{Commit}: a seat holding a hold signs
or walks; a missing or invalid action is a walk, and every hold expires at the end of the phase.
Signing ends seat 0's play; the market ends after round 2 or when every listing is leased.

\textbf{Observation of seat 0.} The board (asks, uninformative listing attributes, open or leased),
its rejected listings and active hold, its values v, the lemon count and share, the loss, the
fee, its own inspections and spend, and \texttt{your\_\allowbreak{}history}: its own offers, each hold with the
binding rent and whether the landlord accepted or countered, and each sign or walk. The sealed
prompt states the payoff, that a fixed landlord ``may accept or make one binding counteroffer'',
that ``a counteroffer can itself convey information about listing quality'', the departure rule,
that a listing seat 0 bid on is protected that round, the three-round horizon, that walking does
not close a listing, and the order of phases. v14's prompt states neither the landlord's pricing
rule nor the cost range. v15's brief states both: a sound landlord accepts any offer at or above its cost plus \$25
(or the ask, if lower) and otherwise counters there; a sound listing's cost is \$20 to \$80 below its ask; so a rent
more than \$55 below the ask proves a lemon.

\textbf{Example: a reply and a signing.} A listing asks a = \$2,400; its sound cost is c\^{}S = \$2,350, so
a lemon's is c\^{}L = \$1,350, and seat 0 values it at v = \$2,600 if sound. With the \$25 margin
(B.1) a sound listing's landlord targets \$2,375, a lemon's leaky landlord \$1,375, and a lemon's
pooled landlord \$2,375. Seat 0 offers \$2,000. A sound landlord counters at \$2,375; a pooled lemon
landlord also counters at \$2,375; a leaky lemon landlord accepts at \$2,000, and only a lemon's
landlord would, so the acceptance itself marks the lemon. Signing pays v − rent = +\$225 for the
sound listing at \$2,375, v − D − rent = −\$400 for the leaky lemon at \$2,000, and −\$775 for the
pooled lemon at \$2,375; inspecting first costs \$25 and shows the quality under either kind. Had
seat 0 offered \$0, the leaky lemon landlord would have countered at \$1,375, which also marks the
lemon, and signing there pays +\$225.

does not know; that is why the best agreement depends on the other side's type.

\section{Counterpart policies}\label{counterpart-policies}

\subsection{Housing: landlords and outside demand}\label{housing-landlords-and-outside-demand}

\textbf{Landlord.} Each landlord has a reservation ρ\_ℓ: ρ\_ℓ = c\_ℓ (its own cost) under the \emph{leaky}
kind, and ρ\_ℓ = c\^{}S\_ℓ for every listing under the \emph{pooled} kind. Its target is
τ\_ℓ = max(ρ\_ℓ, min(a\_ℓ, ρ\_ℓ + μ)). Given the highest offer x* in its inbox (ties to the lower seat),
it accepts (hold at x*) if x* ≥ τ\_ℓ and otherwise counters at τ\_ℓ. A \$0 offer therefore always
draws a counter at τ\_ℓ. On the main pack a sound listing's target lies in {[}a − 55, a{]}; a lemon's
under the leaky kind in {[}a − 1055, a − 996{]}; a lemon's under the pooled kind equals a sound one's.
A reply reveals quality only to an offer below the sound floor min(a\_ℓ, c\^{}S\_ℓ + 25); any offer at or
above it is accepted by both.

\textbf{Outside demand.} Listing ℓ is wanted in round r iff H(w-asks, ℓ, r)/2⁶⁴ \textless{} β, with H the first
eight bytes of a SHA-256 of the world's asks, the listing and the round. A wanted open listing is
taken: its outside seat offers \$0, the landlord counters at τ\_ℓ, and the seat signs in the same
round. If seat 0 made an offer on ℓ that round, its offer wins the tie and ℓ is not taken. The
schedule depends on neither quality, nor landlord kind, nor seat 0's play; on the main pack 52.1\%
of listing-rounds are takes.

\subsection{Procurement: suppliers}\label{procurement-suppliers}

Quotes carry the true base terms, which equal the listing. Samples are exact. A counter-offer is
accepted only if every term lies between the supplier's limit and the current offer, and every
limit equals the quoted value (price floor = quoted price, minimum order 50, 30-day payment and
refund windows), so only a restatement of the current terms is accepted: quotes are final.
Awards are scored on the true private terms.

\subsection{Data center: the scripted negotiator}\label{data-center-the-scripted-negotiator}

The script plays seat j (the other side is −j), knows its own type exactly, and knows the other side through the
declared types Θ and their prior β₀. Shares (s, s\_min) are fractions of the value on the table Π;
τ, χ and V are money; r, α and κ are dimensionless weights. For a type θ and an agreement c at price p write g\_j and
g₋j for the two sides' surpluses over their outside options, and G(c; θ) = g\_j + g₋j, which does not depend on price.
On each of its moves it takes the five steps below.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Belief.} β ∝ β₀ restricted to types for which every redline the other side made was worth
  at least its outside option to it; an update that would leave no type is skipped.
\item
  \textbf{Value on the table.} Π = max(0, max over c of E\_β G).
\item
  \textbf{Ask.} With N own proposals (3 as integrator, 2 as client) and Δ = (s\_open − s\_final)/(N − 1),
  the ask share starts at s₁ = s\_open and moves to s\_\{k+1\} = max(s\_final, s\_k − Δκ\_k), with
  κ = (1 − r) + r·min(2, max(0, η/Δ)), where η is how far the other side moved toward the script
  since its last proposal, measured as a share of Π. Collapse holds s\_open until its last
  proposal, then asks s\_final.
\item
  \textbf{Proposal.} For each set of the most valuable remaining types, the script picks the clause
  set maximising expected joint value over that set (price only keeps the standing clauses),
  prices it at its break-even plus the ask share of that value, capped at what the least willing
  type in the set would accept, rounds to \$5k, and keeps the set with the largest expected value
  to itself, types outside the set counting as refusing. V is that largest expected value: the script's
  surplus from its best proposal, counting a refusal as 0.
\item
  \textbf{Move.} With τ the expected value to the script of the standing offer and χ = α·V − f the
  value of proposing, where α is how likely it takes its proposal to be signed and f the redline fee (25, or 0
  on move 1), it signs if τ ≥ max(0, s\_min·Π) and either it cannot propose or τ ≥ χ; otherwise
  it redlines if it has a proposal with χ \textgreater{} 0 (a price-only script keeps redlining while it has a proposal);
  otherwise it walks. It never reads or writes a note.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reference policies and baselines}\label{reference-policies-and-baselines}

The references are of three kinds, and a shortfall means something different against each. A
\emph{reachable policy} (Housing's \texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak{}lowball}) uses only what the deciding seat may know; it is
a witness to what is achievable, not an optimum. The \emph{best policy given the decider's
information} (Procurement) is the exact optimum over what the buyer can learn, so a shortfall
against it is the whole cost of the subject's decisions. A \emph{full-information upper bound}
(Housing's ceiling, the data-center best agreement) uses hidden state that no seat sees, so a
shortfall against it also contains the value of information no seat had.

\subsection{Housing}\label{housing}

\textbf{Reachable reference (\texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak{}lowball}).} Each round: if no open, verified-sound listing exists,
inspect the uninspected open listing with the largest v − a \textgreater{} 0; offer \$0 on a listing it has inspected and that
is still open, choosing a sound one if it has one and otherwise a lemon, and among those the one with the largest
v − a; sign iff the listing was inspected
and its known value (v, or v − D for a lemon) covers the rent. It never signs blind and never
weighs the fee. It earns \$291.67 a world on the main pack.

\textbf{Ceiling.} C(w, kind) = max(0, max\_ℓ {[}v\_ℓ − D·𝟙{[}ℓ ∈ Λ{]} − τ\_ℓ\^{}kind{]}), with τ\_ℓ\^{}kind the landlord's target under that kind (B.1): known quality, no fee,
reachable in round 0. \$447.82 (leaky), \$292.40 (pooled), mean \$370.11.

the best verified-sound listing and signs it (\$144.70). \texttt{sign\_\allowbreak{}anything} never inspects,
offers the ask on its favourite listing if sound, and signs any hold covered by its value if
sound: −\$79.53 under either decision-value measure, but −\$348.76 in realised net. \texttt{pass}
never acts (\$0). The decision-value measure is the one used for the control comparisons in
Section 5 and Appendix E.4.

\subsection{Data center}\label{data-center}

\textbf{Best agreement.} J(c) = IC(c) + C\_C(c); c* = argmin over the 600 agreements of J (ties to the
draft, then a fixed order). The value available is A = max(0, O\_C − O\_I − J(c*)).

\textbf{Pairs.} The \emph{oracle pair} has the integrator propose c* at IC* + O\_I + A/2 (or walk if A ≤ 0) and the
client sign: joint value lost 0 on all 58 worlds. The \emph{draft pair} proposes c⁰ unchanged at its
midpoint: \$318k a world lost over the 58.

\textbf{Stand-ins against a script} (they know both true types and always redline to c* at share h of A):
\emph{takes the first offer} signs the script's opening (as client) or its first counter (as integrator)
if it is worth at least walking away, and earns 114 and 122; \emph{best plan} searches every sequence of
\{walk, sign, c* at h ∈ \{0.1, \ldots, 1.0\}\} against the deterministic script with its declared style and
keeps the best for itself net of fees, an exact best reply within that grid and a ceiling, not a
target.

\textbf{Best informed policy turn by turn} (computed after the runs). For either seat, it is the best play on what that
seat knows: its own costs, the declared odds of the other side's types, and the script's rule and style. It is
found by backward induction over the turns and the types still consistent with every script move so far.
- \textbf{Its moves.} Walk, sign, or redline to the agreement worth most in expectation to some set of live types (or the
standing terms), at a price on a grid of each type's room. At the integrator's last redline it also tries the
highest price at which the client script still signs.
- \textbf{Checks.} Every plan replays against the original script.
- \textbf{It is a lower bound.} It is the best within that class. Of 720 model plays across both confirmatories, 2
integrator and 7 client plays earn more than the same search told the type.
- \textbf{Loss by turn.} Each model move is charged its expected value against this policy, given what the model's side had
seen.
- \textbf{What it shows.} Knowing the other side's type is worth \$0.1k to \$2.7k a world to this policy, in either seat and
either campaign.

\section{Measures}\label{measures}

Each measure answers one question in the case's own units.

\textbf{D.1 Housing.} \emph{What did the tenant's decisions earn, with luck taken out?} The measure is the
tenant's net in dollars, except that a listing signed without knowing its quality is valued at what
the tenant could expect from it rather than at the quality that happened to be drawn. A decision is
\emph{blind} if the listing was not inspected when seat 0 committed; its
stated lemon probability is p\_d = (2 − lemons found)/(4 − listings inspected). A blind signing is
valued at v − D when the landlord is leaky and the rent is below the listing's sound floor, and at
v − D·p\_d otherwise. Per cell, N = (v\_true − rent) for an informed signing (v\_true is v − D for a lemon and v for a sound listing) or (value − rent) for a
blind one, less c·k; per world, Y\_w = ½(N\_leaky + N\_pooled), a world missing either kind being
dropped. Reported: the mean of Y\_w over the main pack with a 95\% Student-t interval; model pairs
paired by world, Holm-adjusted over three; each model minus the reachable reference; \$50 as the
smallest meaningful difference. A counter at the sound floor proves a listing sound but is still
valued at p\_d, and a lemon revealed on one listing does not update p\_d on another. The
\emph{corrected measure} (\texttt{price\_\allowbreak{}reply\_\allowbreak{}posterior}) values every blind signing at v − D·P(ℓ ∈ Λ \textbar{} the
inspections and every informative reply); in the v14 cells it moves 57 signings and no declared
verdict, and the second gap (a lemon revealed on one listing) has no effect on these data. Table 5.2 reports both.

\textbf{D.2 Procurement.} \emph{Where in the episode was value lost, and how much?} Each action is charged
what it gave up against the best action available at that point, given what the buyer had
learned; the charges add up to the play's shortfall in expected value, so the measure says both
how much was lost and at which step. For each action taken, ℓ\textsubscript{t} = V(s\textsubscript{t}) − Q(s\textsubscript{t}, a\textsubscript{t}), and a play's decision loss is
DL = Σ\textsubscript{t} ℓ\textsubscript{t}. For inquire, counter and check, Q = −c + V(σ, n − 1, t + d); for defer or an invalid
action Q = D, the value of supplying nothing (−\$600, Table A.2); for an award, Q is its margin at I = 0, or D if infeasible. Because every Q is an
expectation, E{[}DL(π){]} = V*(s₀) − V\^{}π(s₀). DL is reported in five parts: requests, strays (inquiries, counters and award checks), walking
away (defer or an invalid action), award timing (V less the best award available) and award terms (the best award available less
the award taken).

\textbf{D.3 Data center.} \emph{How much of what the best agreement makes available did the pair fail to
realise?} Value is lost by settling on worse clauses, by failing to agree, or by paying for
redlines; how the remainder is split between the sides is reported separately, as each side's
surplus. Joint value lost = A − (U\_I + U\_C), the sum of three parts: allocation
J(c) − min(J(c*), O\_C − O\_I) if signed; no deal, A, if nothing is signed (an invalid move counts as no
deal); and delay, 25·(n\_I + n\_C). Each side's surplus is U\_I or U\_C. A side signs below its walk-away
point if its surplus before redline fees (its U plus 25 times its own redlines) is negative.
