\section{Quality control}\label{quality-control}\label{sec:qc}

The defects that mattered in the four cases were properties of the distribution, the task prompt, the
counterpart or the analysis, and every world was individually correct when they were found. In one, a
seat-number tie-break decided who signed the good listing; in another, a prompt pointed
every agent at the right supplier in half the worlds and at the wrong one in the other half; in a third,
a hidden type was tied to the stratum of the evaluation set, so that comparisons by type compared worlds.
An inspection of one world at a time passes all three. We therefore organise quality control
around populations of worlds, pre-registered analyses and recorded failures. Appendix F describes each
check that we applied to these cases, and only those.

\textbf{Gates.} A case is qualified through six quality-control gates, whose verdicts are recorded in its family's QC
profile. Gate 0 requires that the profile exists; Gate 1, that the worlds are valid, distinct, split and leave headroom;
Gate 2, that actions change the intended state and that every score is rebuilt from sealed evidence; Gate 3, that a
better-informed policy does better and that shortcuts lose; Gate 4, that the result supports the attribution it claims;
and Gate 5, that the comparison was sized, frozen and executed without tuning on outcomes. Each gate has one of five
statuses (\texttt{passed}, \texttt{failed}, \texttt{partial}, \texttt{not\_run} or \texttt{not\_\allowbreak{}applicable}), and only \texttt{passed} qualifies a result. We wrote
the profiles after the initial runs. The confirmatory run of the agreement case came after its profile. Housing and
Procurement pass four of Gates 1 to 5, all except Gate 4, and no case yet passes all five (Table 6.1).

\textbf{Headroom (Gate 1).} When a case computes its reference exactly, as Housing and Procurement do, every shortcut named
in its profile must lose a pre-specified margin to the reference in every world. Otherwise, a frozen model control
screens the worlds. We added the first route on 21 October (Appendix F.1). The data-center agreement's reference is a
lower bound, and its evaluation set is frozen, so its Gate 1 can pass only on a new evaluation set.

\textbf{The reference is played on every world.} For each case, a test plays the reference through the
real environment and verifier on every included world, with no model, and checks that it reaches
its stated result. Every reported evaluation set has such a test; Appendix F.2 lists them, including the ones we
wrote after the runs they check. Two of the tests, the data-center grade and the Housing ceiling, are also shown to
fail when the grade or the reference is wrong.

\textbf{Other checks.} Every committed evaluation set is regenerated byte for byte from its seeds, and the Housing
v14 and v15 receipts replay from their sealed events. We report trivial agents beside the evaluated agents, score
Procurement's fixed rules exactly, and audited the task prompts for instructions that
decide the outcome. Uncertainty is computed over worlds, and every claim names its counterparts. Six
reported analyses were pre-registered: Housing v14 and v15 (with v15's two addenda), Procurement's
main test, the data-center signalling test and the two confirmatory runs of the agreement case. Procurement's
frozen-control diagnostic was also pre-registered before its plays.

\textbf{What it found.} We record every discovered failure in an incident log, as design,
operational, tooling or judgment rows that are never deleted. On 16 October the log held 51 entries about Housing,
57 about Procurement, 90 about the data center and 23 about Refund (Table F.3), and rows have been added since. Of the
25 defects selected for Table F.4, 14 were invisible in any single world. We found the 11 visible ones by reading
code or sealed bytes, by tracing a case, or within a single gate. Nine defects were open when we compiled the
table. A later reanalysis from the sealed actions resolves one of them for the reported panels, which leaves eight
open. The same checks corrected three of our own statements while we wrote this paper.
