\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the cases show}
\label{sec:discussion-findings}

AERead evaluates an agent's economic decisions by the value it obtains for
a principal, given the information, resources and authority of its seat.
Each score is measured against a reference whose access to information is
stated. It keeps its own units and names the counterparts it was measured
against, so every shortfall has a defined interpretation. Our cases apply
this to information acquisition, bargaining and commitment in housing,
procurement and contracting, and a reimplementation of part of TERMS-Bench
runs on the same runtime.

Refund tests a different obligation: compliance with an explicit policy,
not money earned. Separating the four seats, and keeping each diagnostic
score beside its coverage, distinguishes a plausible resolution from an
evidence-complete, correctly executed path. Its developmental calibration
separates Flash Lite from the stronger models in three seats, but the
saturated customer seat and the little room left above the stronger models
limit further comparisons. The scripted conformance of the later release
does not remove the lower-control concerns of Section~\ref{sec:qc}.

The cases teach three lessons that bear on any evaluation of this kind.
The counterpart is part of the measurement: counting each transfer between
them once, the same two agents differ by about three times as much when they
negotiate with each other as when each faces a fixed counterpart. The task prompt is part of the case: stating a rule that the prompt had left
implicit changed scores as much as changing the agent did. And the defects
that matter are often properties of the population of worlds, the task prompt or
the analysis rather than of any single world. Construction and quality
control therefore belong in the method, and the checks of Section 6 changed
the interpretation of our own results. Loss categories locate a shortfall
but do not explain it, and explaining it requires controlled follow-up
comparisons. Replay establishes consistency with recorded play; construct
validity requires evidence about the worlds, the information and the
controls.

These properties matter most where agents are deployed. An organization
that delegates a decision to an agent needs to know what the agent's
choices are worth against what was achievable, and where its losses come
from; a value score against a stated reference and a typed account of
losses answer both questions. Where one person makes such decisions
without specialized expertise or review before a commitment, delegating
them to an agent also requires evidence that the agent keeps value against
the counterparts it will meet.

The present evidence has clear limits. The cases take their decision
structure from informal rentals, wholesale sourcing and AI-infrastructure
contracting, but we set their parameters; calibration to
field data and transfer to field settings remain to be established.
Some parameters carry a result. Housing's leaky landlord passes the whole of a
lemon's discount through to its rent, so a known lemon is exactly as good a
lease as the same listing sound, and the largest Housing shortfall class,
declining such leases, rests on that knife-edge choice. An evaluation set in
which the discount is smaller than the loss would test the adverse-selection
decision that the introduction describes.
Coverage is limited to two transaction classes and a small set of agent
configurations, with no human policy baseline. The data-center comparison tests bargaining
against a fixed scripted counterpart, not inference about the other side's type. No case passes all of the
quality-control gates (Section 6). Several checks were added after the runs
they qualify. The behavior of hosted models and command-line tools also limits
how far a result can be attributed to its stated settings.
Versioned reanalyses now regenerate the corrected Housing measures and
Procurement decision losses from sealed records, which strengthens their
provenance. Full verification against the original execution still
requires the retained logs (Appendix~G). The reanalyses neither qualify the
campaigns retroactively nor resolve Housing's historical seat attribution
(Appendix~H). The results therefore do not establish general model rankings
or readiness for deployment in a particular firm.

\subsection{Open research directions and contributions}
\label{sec:open-directions}

AERead is an open environment for studying how agents make economic
decisions. Its code is open source under the Apache-2.0 license
(\url{https://github.com/aeread-org/AERead}; contact@aeread.org), and contributors can
pursue their own questions with the existing cases, counterparts and
evidence. The results above leave four questions open, each suited to a
different kind of contribution.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Counterpart populations.} In the Data-center case, the gap between two
agents depended strongly on whether they negotiated against each other or each faced a fixed script.
How far does an agent's measured skill depend on whom it faces, can
rankings reverse across counterpart populations (none did in our runs),
and which populations separate distinct strategic capabilities? The
scripted styles, head-to-head pairings and counterpart seats are in place.
\item \textbf{Validity of populations of worlds.} More than half of the defects
we selected from our record were invisible in any single world. Which checks find
such defects before a campaign runs, and what should qualify a case? The
quality-control gates, the incident record and the failed campaigns are
available for study.
\item \textbf{Information and judgment.} Stating a rule that the task prompt had left
implicit changed scores as much as changing the agent. Separating what an
agent was told from how it decided, and computing best-informed references
for richer cases, are open methodological problems.
\item \textbf{New economic decisions.} A case for another activity
supplies its rules, worlds and reference; execution, replay, counterpart
seats and the quality-control standard come with the runtime.
\end{itemize}

Some components are ready and others are not. The runtime, its sealed evidence and
replay, and the case layout are in use, and Housing, Procurement and Data
center each have a world generator, references and scripted counterparts.
No case is yet fully qualified. Section~\ref{sec:qc} gives every gate
verdict, and Appendix~F lists each campaign's open checks, so a contributor
can see what a case still needs before its results can rank agents.

The next steps follow from the limits in Section 7.1. Future campaigns should bind
the actual seat assignments and headline scorers into frozen evaluation
configurations and run the required controls before any comparison, and the
TERMS-Bench reimplementation needs the protocol fixes described in
Appendix~E.10 before its pre-registered reproduction can proceed. Practitioner review would
calibrate the cases' parameters, human baselines would show where agents
stand against people, and externally contributed cases would test whether
the structure serves authors other than its maintainers. AERead's present
contribution is that structure, a common way to construct evaluations of
economic decisions, together with an evidence-based account of the checks
needed to interpret what they measure.
